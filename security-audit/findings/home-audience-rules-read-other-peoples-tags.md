Description: Heliosian's audience rules kept whatever `owner` the request named (`home.checkRules`), so a Heliosian admin - who is nobody in Who? - read any member's private tags by name: who is in them, through the preview, and which names exist.
Status: fixed
Severity: medium
---
A rule read its tags as its `Owner`, a field the client set, and Heliosian never asked whether the caller could read as that owner. `POST /api/apps/audience/preview` with `{"rules":[{"kind":"include","owner":"victim@heliosschool.org","tags":["Carpool"]}]}` answered the count and twelve names; adding search words walked the rest, and the labels told a real tag from a guess. Saved on a link or a section, the rule went on reading the victim's tag. Loop guarded the same thing with checks of its own (`ownRules`, `sharedRules`); When's guest lists set the owner to the host.

Fixed by taking the owner out of the rule altogether (`internal/filter`): a rule names a tag absolutely, the owner's address and the name, and `filter.Writable` - called by Loop's preview and save, Heliosian's preview and three saves, and When's preview and add - refuses a rule naming a tag the saver does not own or manage, with one message whether the tag is someone else's or does not exist. A rule already on the thing word for word passes, so a colleague's rule survives an edit to the rest. A tag then reads while one of the thing's editors - a group's managers, Heliosian's admins, an event's hosts (`Editors` on `filter.List`) - may read it. Loop's own checks are deleted in favour of the one, and Loop's group pages send the rules and each member's reasons to managers and admins alone. `docs/loop/loop.md`, A rule discloses the tags it names, says the rule once; `TestWritable` in `internal/filter` and the preview refusals in `internal/calendar/invites_test.go` hold it.
