Description: Helios Ask's mail hook files a message as list mail, a school newsletter or an announcement on the strength of headers its sender wrote - `List-Id`, `From`, or `To` and `Cc` (`artifacts.Channel`) - checking nothing about who sent it or how it came, so one mail to the forwarding mailbox, forged or merely addressed the right way, puts an outsider's text into every member's answers and system prompt.
Status: revisit
Severity: high
---
`internal/artifacts/inbox.go:66-164` verifies Mailgun's signature on the notification and then reads the message; no SPF, DKIM or DMARC result is looked at. `Channel` (`internal/artifacts/channel.go:22-45`) decides kind and audience in three branches, each from a header the sender wrote:

- `List-Id` (`:24-29`): `List-Id: <parents.heliosschool.org>` files as list mail to that list. The header is the sender's to write.
- `From` (`:35-37`): `mailer` (`:54-61`) is a substring match over the whole lowered `From` header, so `From: "veracross.com" <x@elsewhere.example>` or `x@veracross.com.elsewhere.example` files as a `newsletter`, with nothing forged that a receiving server would refuse. The same match runs over a `List-Id` (`:30-32`).
- `To` and `Cc` (`:38-43`): a broadcast address among the recipients files as an `announcement`. Nothing is forged at all: the mail is written `To: parents@heliosschool.org` and delivered to the forwarding parent's own address.

All three answer every asker. The title of a recent document goes into every conversation's system prompt (`internal/ask/prompt.go`), and `internal/ask/prompt.md` tells Claude a recent document can override the calendar. `known` (`inbox.go:173-180`) also skips a newsletter whose title and day are on file, so a forgery sent first displaces the real issue.

A mail to the forwarding parent's school address, shaped any of the three ways, with the subject "School closed Monday" is then the latest word when a member asks whether there is school on Monday; it is also where injected instructions for the model would go. Nothing bounds its size short of 64 MB, each 6000 characters an embedding call and a vector kept in memory. Proving the `From` closes only the second branch: in the first and third the sender's `From` is honestly their own and passes DMARC.

Fix: match mailers on the parsed `From` address's domain, by suffix, and file a newsletter only when that `From` is proven, by the test Helios Loop already holds its posts to (`authenticated`, `internal/loop/rewrite.go:79`, over Mailgun's topmost `Authentication-Results` and, for forwarded mail, Google's sealed ARC results), shared rather than written twice. List mail and announcements need proof that the message came through the school's list rather than straight to the mailbox, which the headers of a real forwarded message - what the list's server signs, what the forwarding mailbox seals - have to be read to settle. Cap the message's length before embedding.

Waiting on: `logHeaders` (`inbox.go`) logs, as `artifacts: mail headers`, what each message the hook takes carries - the list, sender and forwarding headers, every `Authentication-Results`, `ARC-Authentication-Results` and `ARC-Seal`, and what `Channel` made of it. A week or so of those lines says whether the forwarding mailbox's seal and Mailgun's `arc=pass` are there, whether the mailers' DKIM survives the forward aligned with their `From`, what the school's lists sign, and which real mail takes the third branch - mail to a broadcast address that reached the mailbox another way, by a direct copy or through `hca@`, looks likely. The fix is designed from that, and `logHeaders` goes with it.
