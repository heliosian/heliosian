Description: Helios When records a calendar reply received by mail as the attendee's answer on the strength of the message's `From` line alone (`calendar.takeReply`), so anyone who can send mail can accept or decline any event as any member or invited guest.
Status: fixed
Severity: high
---
With only Mailgun's signature on the notification checked - that Mailgun called, and nothing about the message - a mail to the reply address with `From: victim@heliosschool.org` and a `text/calendar` part holding `METHOD:REPLY`, `UID:<event id>@calendar.heliosian.com` and `ATTENDEE;PARTSTAT=DECLINED:mailto:victim@heliosschool.org` marks the victim as not coming, and `ACCEPTED` puts them on a guest list; either way the hosts are mailed the answer as the victim's (`recordBy`, `internal/calendar/rsvp.go`), on any event `eventFor` finds, which is every public event and every invite-only one by id. The victim is mailed nothing.

Fixed by holding a reply to two tests, both required (`internal/calendar/replies.go`, `replies` and `takeReply`):

- The sender is authenticated. The test Helios Loop holds a post to lives in `internal/mail/headers.go` as `mail.Authenticated` - Mailgun's topmost `Authentication-Results` passing DMARC, DKIM or SPF aligned with the `From` domain, or Google's ARC-sealed results for mail Google forwarded - and Loop and the calendar both call it. The `From` it authenticates is the raw message's first `From` line, and that same value is what `takeReply` matches against the attendee; the notification's `from` field decides nothing.
- The reply came to the attendee's own address for the event. Every invite, update and cancellation names `when+<token>@reply.heliosian.com` as its organizer and as the last of its reply addresses (`organizer`, `internal/calendar/rsvp.go`), the token being `replyToken`: thirty-two hex characters of HMAC-SHA256 over the event id and the attendee's address under the session key (`calendar.Mail.Key`). `takeReply` recomputes it for the reply's event and attendee and compares in constant time with the tag on the notification's recipient.

So a forger needs both to pass SPF or DKIM as the victim's domain and to hold the victim's invite for that event; a forwarded invite gives its holder nothing, since they cannot send as the attendee. A reply from a domain that publishes neither SPF nor DKIM, and a reply to the bare address - an invite sent before the token, or a calendar app answering the sender rather than the organizer - are logged and dropped, and the person answers on the page. `TestRepliesRecordAnswers` and `TestRepliesFromGuests` cover each refusal.
