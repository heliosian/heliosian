# Helios Wiki

Helios Wiki, at `wiki.heliosian.com`, is parent-to-parent info: a tree of pages anyone in the community writes and anyone signed in reads. It is an app like any other in `model.Apps`, so its place on Heliosian and the app switch follows its row on the Apps sheet's `Visibility` tab, and a new deployment finds no row and adds one listing nobody (`docs/home/data.md`).

It is built on the data model alone (`docs/datamodel.md`): it reads through `QUERY /api/q` and writes through `/api/do/wiki` and `POST /api/q`, as the signed-in person, so the policies decide everything it shows and allows.

## Pages

A page is a `DOCUMENT` of kind `wiki`: its `name` is the title, `author` the person who started it, `published` when they did, and `content` a `CONTENT` row of type `text/markdown` holding the page's Markdown. The extractor, search and inbox leave wiki pages alone.

Pages form a tree. A page's `parent` is the wiki page it sits under, blank for a page at the top, and `order` its place among its siblings. A sub-page keeps its kind, the one exception to the data model's rule that a document under another has a relation and no kind; the load refuses a wiki page under any document that is no wiki page, and a page whose parents lead back round to itself (`checkDocuments` in `internal/db/model.go`).

A page's address is `/p/` and one part for each page from the top of its section down to it, the page's own last: `wiki.heliosian.com/p/tours/Getting-Started/First-Week`. A page's part is its `slug` when it has one, and otherwise its title with every run of anything but letters and digits made a single hyphen, capitals kept (`wikiSegment` and `WikiPath` in `internal/db/wiki.go`, `segmentOf` and `pagePath` in `web/wiki/state.js`), so every page has a readable address without anyone choosing one, and a slug is a short override for one part. Every link to a page uses that address, and `/p/{id}` keeps working too. A slug is lowercase letters, digits and single hyphens, at most 40, and never shaped like an id, so a slug and an id can't be confused under `/p/`. Addresses match whatever the case, so no two pages under the same parent may have parts that differ only in case: a save that would give a page the part a sibling has is refused, and the load refuses two siblings with the same slug, or a slug on a document that is no wiki page (`checkWikiSlug`). Any change that moves a page's address - a new title, a new or removed slug, a new parent - writes a `REDIRECT` row, `app` `wiki`, from the old address to `/p/` and the page's id (`wikiAddressMoved` in `internal/db/trigger.go`), a trigger, so it holds for any write. Its sub-pages need no rows of their own: a page is found by its id, or part by part from the top, each part a page under the one found so far, else a redirect from the address up to that part as written, else a redirect from the address of the page found so far and that part (`wikiPageAt`), so an old address under a moved page keeps opening its page, and the address bar is corrected to the live one.

A page may be `hidden`: kept out of the wiki's lists of pages - the front page, search, the rail and Pages in this section - for all but its author and the wiki's admins, its sub-pages going with it. It is only kept out of the lists: everyone signed in still reads it, so its link opens it for anyone. The load refuses `hidden` on a document that is no wiki page.

A page may have side cards, shown down its right: each a `DOCUMENT` under the page with the relation `side`, its `name` the card's title, its `content` the card's Markdown, and its `order` its place among the page's cards. The load refuses a side card under anything but a wiki page. Cards are the page's alone: its sub-pages don't show them.

A page's or a card's Markdown may show pictures, `![words](/api/wiki/picture/<sha256>.<ext>)`: a picture goes up, and comes in from the picture libraries, through the same image routes HCA-Team, Celebrate and When use (`imagesearch.Search.Register`, under `/api/wiki`, the folder `wiki-images` from `imageFolders` in `internal/blob/images.go`), into `wiki-images/` in the media bucket, named by its SHA-256, and `GET /api/wiki/picture/{name}` (`registerWiki` in `internal/db/wiki.go`) answers it from the bucket to anyone the directory signs in, for a name the `wiki-images/` pattern allows and nothing else, under a `Content-Security-Policy: sandbox` of its own. The shared `markdown.js` draws an image only from that path, so Markdown anywhere, a wiki page or an Ask answer, never loads a picture from elsewhere; any other image is drawn as its words.

A list item indented further than the one above it starts a list inside that item, bulleted or numbered by its own mark, so lists nest to any depth. A quote, lines starting `> `, holds Markdown of its own - paragraphs split by a bare `>` line, lists, line breaks - drawn by `markdown.js` inside the quote. Markdown may hold callouts, GitHub's alert syntax: a blockquote whose first line is `> [!NOTE]`, `> [!TIP]`, `> [!IMPORTANT]`, `> [!WARNING]` or `> [!CAUTION]` alone, its following `>` lines the callout's own Markdown, drawn by `markdown.js` as a box with the kind's icon and name, in the kind's colour (`web/common/markdown.css`, its colours `--callout-<kind>` in the palette and `dark.css`). A wiki page and an Ask answer both draw them.

```markdown
> [!WARNING]
> The trail closes at dusk.
>
> - Bring a headlamp
> - Bring a warm layer
```

A page may have a header image, a banner across its top, held in YAML front matter at the very start of its Markdown, so it is the page's data and needs nothing in the data model of its own:

```markdown
---
header_image: /api/wiki/picture/<sha256>.<ext>
---
The page's Markdown.
```

The client reads `header_image` only when it names a picture under `/api/wiki/picture/`, for the same reason `markdown.js` draws no other image, and draws the Markdown after the front matter. The link preview's first sentence skips the front matter too (`withoutFrontMatter` in `internal/db/wikishare.go`). A page with no header image of its own wears the nearest page above it that has one (`headerFor` in `web/wiki/state.js`), and its Edit image menu still sets the page's own; a page with none above it either wears one of the default pictures bundled in `web/wiki/backgrounds/` instead, chosen by a hash of its top-level page's id, so a page always gets the same one and every page in a section shares it (`defaultHeader` in `web/wiki/state.js`); nothing is stored for it, and choosing a picture replaces it.

`POST /api/do/wiki` takes `{"document", "parent", "slug", "name", "body", "sides"}` and saves a page: with no `document` it starts one under `parent`, last among its siblings; otherwise it renames and rewrites that one and, when `parent` differs from the page's own, moves it there, last among its new siblings, and when `slug` differs, sets it, so every save names the parent and slug the page should have. A parent that is no wiki page, or that is the page itself or one of its own sub-pages, is refused, as is a slug the load would refuse, or a title or slug that would give the page the address a sibling has. `sides`, when sent, is every card the page should have, in order, each `{"id", "name", "body"}`: a card with no `id` is added, one with an `id` of the page's is retitled, rewritten and placed as it says, and a card of the page's that the list leaves out is deleted; a save without `sides` leaves the cards as they are. The call stores every body as below, the body in the media bucket under `content/` and its `CONTENT` row, unless a row with the same bytes already exists, and in the same transaction inserts or sets the `DOCUMENT` as the caller, answering `{"result", "hash"}`. The `CONTENT` row is the server's to make: no policy lets a person insert one, so a page's bytes are always what was sent. Each save points the page at its new content; the previous content, held by nothing, goes with the sweeper, so a page keeps no history of its own. Reordering is a set of the page's `order` through `POST /api/q`.

## Who may do what

The clauses are in `policySource` (`internal/db/policy.go`), under Everyone, Wiki admins and System: import:

- Anyone signed in reads every page, as for any document sent to no group.
- Anyone but a guest starts a page, as its author, giving it a slug or not, and renames, rewrites, moves or reorders any page, and adds, changes, reorders or removes its side cards. Its content must be Markdown that no document but a wiki page holds.
- The import key (`docs/datamodel.md`, The query API) starts pages with no author, and their side cards, for loading a document into the wiki; since it is no guest, the clauses above let it rename, rewrite and move pages too.
- A page's author, and a wiki admin, hide it or make it public again through its `hidden`.
- A page's author, and a wiki admin, give it a slug, change it or take it away; anyone else's save keeps the page's slug as it is. A page with no author, as the import makes, gets its slug from a wiki admin.
- Anyone signed in reads the `wiki` rows of `REDIRECT`, so an old address finds its page; the other apps' redirects stay a super admin's.
- A page with no sub-pages is deleted by its author, and by a wiki admin, its side cards deleted with it in the same batch. A page with sub-pages can't be deleted until they are moved or deleted. A wiki admin is an effective member of the `admins` group the `wiki` `APP` row names, or a super admin.

## The app

`web/wiki/` is the client, served for `/`, `/new` and everything under `/p/` (`internal/app`): a page's address, live or old, or its id, and either with `/edit` after it for the editor. A page whose own address ends in a part `edit` opens as a page, its editor one `/edit` further. No page says who started it or when.

- The rail is laid out as HCA-Team's is: main items with an icon, and the rows under each behind a rule. On a page, the first main item is the top-level page that page sits in, with everything under it, each branch folded but for the open page's and what the viewer opened, each fold remembered in the browser. Then All Pages, a link to `/`, which lists the top-level pages; the rail lists none under it. New Page, a small button, closes the rail. A star marks each page the viewer started. Dragging a page in the open section above or below a sibling reorders it.
- `/` lists the top-level pages as cards, as Celebrate lists parties (`card` in `web/common/cardgrid.js`, `docs/dev.md`): the page's header image, as the page wears it; a yellow Yours with a star on a page the viewer started; Hidden in grey on a hidden page, and how many pages it holds; the title, the page's first sentence, as its link preview has it, or, for a page with no text, the titles of its sub-pages, and Open. A search in the top bar lists every page whose title holds the words the same way, with its path under the title.
- A page is laid out as Celebrate lays out a party. Across the top, the path to the page on one row with, for its author or a wiki admin, Public or Hidden with an eye, which switches the page's `hidden` to the other; Add Sub-Page; and Edit Page. Under it, the page's header image, its nearest ancestor's or its default picture, in the banner the other apps' event pages wear (`detailHero` in `web/common/heroimage.js`, `docs/dev.md`): the picture, opened full size on a click, with Share at its corner and the Edit image menu along its foot (upload, find one in the picture libraries, crop, remove; crop and remove only over the page's own picture). Choosing a picture there saves it at once: the client reloads the model, so it starts from the page's latest content, and saves that Markdown with the front matter rewritten, sending no `sides` (`setHeader` in `web/wiki/state.js`). Find an image is always offered, since the wiki has no settings resource to say whether the server has picture-library keys; without them the search answers that there is nowhere to search. Then its title, its Markdown drawn by the shared `markdown.js` and the pages under it; beside them, On this page lists the page's headings, `#`, `##` and `###` (drawn by `markdown.js` as `h2` to `h4`, under the page's own `h1` title, each a step smaller), each indented by its level under the page's highest and scrolling to its heading, and the page's side cards follow it. Under the page's Markdown, Back Up names and opens the page above it, or the wiki's front page for a page at the top, and Next Page names and opens the page after it in the tree as the rail lists it: its first sub-page, else its next sibling, else the next sibling of the nearest page above it that has one (`nextPage` in `web/wiki/pages.js`); the last page in the wiki has no Next Page. The page knows the viewer is a wiki admin by asking for the viewer's own `PERSON` row under `(admin_of "wiki")`.
- `/new` (with `?parent=` for a sub-page) and `/p/{id}/edit` are the editor: the header image, with the banner's Edit image menu along its foot (`heroImageBar`: upload, find, crop, remove), which the editor writes into the front matter on Save; the title, the page it sits under, for a new page, the viewer's own or a wiki admin, its address - the address of the page it sits under, then a box for its slug, which, left blank, shows greyed the part its title gives - the Markdown with a toolbar over it (`textToolbar` in `web/wiki/pages.js`): Bold and Italic wrap the selection in `**` or `*`, List puts `- ` before each selected line, Tab and Shift+Tab in the text indent and outdent the selected list lines by two spaces (anywhere else Tab moves on as usual), Link opens a window for the words and the address and puts `[words](address)` in place of the selection, Callout turns the selected lines into a plain quote (Quote) or a callout of the kind chosen (Tip, Important, Warning or Caution), and Insert Image uploads a picture and puts its Markdown at the cursor (each side card has Insert Image as an icon); the toolbar edits through the browser's own insert, so Undo takes each change back, then Save and Cancel, and beside them, as on the page, the side cards, each a title and Markdown that moves up or down or goes, with Add Card; a card left with neither a title nor any Markdown is dropped when the page is saved; and on an existing page, for its author or a wiki admin, Delete.

A link to a page previews in chat apps and social feeds even though the wiki is behind sign-in, as a party's does on Celebrate (`docs/celebrate/celebrate.md`, Sign-in and install): the sign-in page served at a page's address carries the page's Open Graph tags - its title, its live address, and as the description the first sentence of its Markdown, skipping headings, pictures and code, followed by the titles of its sub-pages - and `/open/share/{id}.png` is a public 1200x630 card drawn by `internal/sharecard`: on the left the wiki's logo (`web/public/wiki/brand/logo-lockup-horizontal.png`), then, for a page under another, the title of the top-level page it sits in, bold in the accent colour above its own title (the card's `Section`), shrinking and then wrapping to a second line to show whole, then that sentence, left off when the outline starts with it, and the page's outline as bullets: its sub-pages' titles, or, for a page with none, its headings and the questions of its question callouts, wherever they sit, quotes and callouts included (`pageOutline`); four at most (the fourth says how many more there are when there are), all one size, each cut short with an ellipsis rather than run under the picture. The description ends with the same outline, after In this section or On this page. Neither carries Markdown: quote marks, callout markers, heading marks, emphasis and link addresses are taken out (`markdownLines`); and on the right the header image the page wears, its own, its nearest ancestor's or its default picture, chosen as `headerFor` chooses it, filling the side (`WikiShare` in `internal/db/wikishare.go`, whose `backgroundHash` is `defaultHeader`'s hash). Only the titles, that one sentence and the header image leave sign-in, never the rest of the page; any other address gets the plain sign-in page and no card.

The sample server's wiki is in `sampledata/datadocuments/` (`DOCUMENT` and `CONTENT`), with the pages' Markdown in `sampledata/bucket/content/` (`docs/dev.md`): a fictional tree with sections, sub-pages, a slug on Getting Started, Activities hidden, and a side card on Camping Trips.
