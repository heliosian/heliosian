# Helios Celebrate

Helios Celebrate is the site for the community's fun(d)raiser parties: members of the community host a party - a fondue night, a pickleball mixer, a wine tasting - and families take tickets, which the school invoices later. It serves at celebrate.heliosian.com. The sheet is `Celebrate`, the package is `internal/celebrate`, and the API is `/api/celebrate/`.

## Entities

- **Celebration** - one year's Spring Celebration, the gala the parties raise money around: a code (`SC-2026`), title, theme, when and where, a blurb, a picture, and a button (text and link - Learn More, to the celebration's own site). Parties are filed under one. The one marked Current is the banner across the top of the parties page - its picture as the background under a teal wash, the title and theme, the date and place, and the button - and the others are reachable from the year picker beside the heading. Admins change the banner from Admin Tools › Banner, which previews it and opens the current celebration's editor.
- **Party** - what it is (title, subtitle, a one-line summary for the card, the description, a need-to-know line), who hosts it, a category and an audience line ("Parents & Kids"), when and where, the price and what one ticket covers ("$65 per person"), how many tickets there are (or no limit) and any minimum to hold it, a picture, and its switches (below).
- **Host** - someone who runs a party: they edit it, see who is coming and their contact details, add and remove anyone, and offer waitlist places. A party may have several.
- **Ticket** - one person on a party: someone in the directory by address, or a guest by name - a visiting cousin, a non-Helios sibling, a friend. Each carries who is billed, the price when it was taken, an optional note, and where the invoice stands. A ticket is either sold or a place on the waitlist.

Names, faces, grades and households come from the directory model at request time, like the volunteer portal; the sheet stores only emails. A ticket holder the directory does not know is shown as a guest.

The directory reads the site back: a host's Lists in Helios Who? carry one list per party they host that has not happened yet - its sold tickets' holders and whoever is billed for them, guests by name counted but not listed (`docs/who/directory.md`). Helios When lists every open party on the calendar, and on a party's page there its hosts keep a guest list - the ticket holders and anyone else - and send RSVP invites, so who has a ticket and who has said they are coming stand side by side (`docs/calendar/calendar.md`, Invitations); the answers live in the calendar's sheet, not this one. The party page here joins the two for its hosts: while the party is still to come, a highlighted card leads the rail - **Invite your guests** with Create Invite before a list exists, **Your invite is waiting** with View the invite once one is started and unsent, **RSVPs on Helios When** with See the RSVPs once the invites are out (`Started` and `Invited` in the view) - and the same button sits beside Attendee contact info over Who's Coming; Create Invite sends them to the party's page on When with the list started - the invitation, and a group for the ticket holders, everyone on it unsent until the host sends the invitations there; each ticket sold after goes on the list too, sent its invite on its own while Auto-invite is on and otherwise waiting in Pending (`/e/celebrate/{id}?invite=1`); once the invites have gone out it reads **RSVPs on Helios When** instead, each face on Who's Coming wears its answer - RSVP: Yes, Maybe, No, or No RSVP yet - and the attendee contact list gains an RSVP column (`RenderWith`, over `RSVPLookup`, which the calendar's cache answers; hosts and admins alone).

## The switches

Every party has, on its page for whoever runs it and in its editor:

- **Tickets on sale** - off, the party is listed but sells nothing.
- **Waitlist when full** - a full party takes names in order; off, it shows Sold Out.
- **Adults / Students** - who may hold a ticket: adults are parents and staff alike. A guest fits wherever anyone does, since the host reads the name. At least one must be on.
- **Drop-off** - a child may come without a parent.
- **Parent ticket required** - a parent who stays needs a ticket of their own.

## Availability

What a party's ticket button says follows from the row and the clock: **past** once its end (or start, with no end) is behind; **closed** while it is not Open or sales are off; **available** while tickets remain; **waitlist** when full and taking names; **sold out** when full without one. A party with no cap is never full.

## Who may do what

- **Anyone signed in** browses every open party and sees who is coming. A student who is nothing else browses and no more: tickets are taken, waitlists joined and tickets passed on by a parent (the page says so where the button would be, and the server refuses a student regardless). Everyone else sees names and faces. They take tickets for themselves and their household - the partner and children the directory lists with them - and guests by name (with an address if they have one; the form says who a guest can be - a child where only students come, an adult where only adults do), billed to an adult in the family (a student's tickets go to a parent). Nobody holds two tickets to one party. Once a party is full it sells nothing more: a family **joins the waitlist** instead, which is a request, not a purchase - how many tickets they would like, and a note - billed nowhere until a host offers the places; asking again changes the request. A sold ticket stays sold - it is a fundraiser: no refunds, resell it to another family - so a family cannot give one back; they can **reassign** it (Reassign, beside their ticket) to someone else the party admits - a sibling from the directory, another family it was resold to, or a guest by name - and the ticket keeps its price, its place in line and who is billed - a resale is settled between the two families themselves, never through the invoice. They can leave a waitlist, which cost nothing. Whoever runs the party removes anyone, and reassigns anyone's ticket too.
- **Anyone signed in**, while the Hosting Open setting is on (Admin Tools › Settings; off, Host a Party goes away and the server refuses a new party from anyone but an admin), posts a party with Host a Party. It lands Pending, with them as its host, until an admin opens it; they can edit it meanwhile and it shows on their Hosting page.
- **Hosts** edit their party in place - the pencil on the banner turns edit mode on, and every section grows a pencil: the picture (upload, find, crop, remove), the title, subtitle, description, card summary and need-to-know callout (its emoji - picked from a grid behind the field's caret, or typed - title and words together), who it is for, the date and time, where, the price and tickets; Edit all fields opens the full editor in tabs - Basics (title, subtitle, summary, description, the callout behind a Create Callout button, boxed and tinted as the callout is, with Remove to take it off, and the friendly address), Design (the banner and the flyer), When & where, Tickets (price, capacity, the switches, and the audience words for the card), Hosts (where hosts are added and removed, never themselves), and for an admin alone Admin (status, celebration, category). The status, the year and the category are an admin's alone; the friendly address is the hosts' to choose, as a co-chair's is on HCA-Team, and one that is taken or malformed is refused on Save with the reason under the form. Hosts flip the switches on the party page, open any attendee's ticket, add anyone from the directory (billed to that person, or a student's parent - nobody bills a family outside their own), and **offer** a waitlist request its tickets when places open up: the family gets that many (or fewer, if the host says), the first for whoever asked when the party admits them, the rest as guests of theirs to be named with Reassign - the waitlist is offered by hand, never promoted on its own. A host is never refused for room.
- **Admins**, the Admins tab plus the platform super admins (`docs/config.md`), approve, hide and delete parties (an empty one only), edit any, manage celebrations (which one's parties lead the page, and which one the top banner advertises - two separate flags), categories, settings and the admin list from `/admin`, and read the INVOICING ledger there - the sheet's own accounting tab, grouped by who is billed, with a party filter, an invoiced / not-yet filter and a search, and the same as a CSV; the app writes a row when a ticket sells and accounting fills in the invoice number and who it went to in the sheet, which is what shows. Their hat is off by default: the page treats a system admin as any other parent (plus whatever they host) until they turn on **Super Admin Mode** with the pencil in the toolbar (`docs/toolbar.md`), which brings every admin control back and is remembered per browser; Admin Tools stays in the menu either way. The server sends an admin every party, but with the hat off the page drops a Pending or Hidden one they do not host - off the lists, the tabs' and rail's counts and the Hosting page, and its address says it is not here, as it would to any parent - and Who's Coming wears no RSVPs; the hat on brings both back. Approvals are the exception, since a party waiting on one waits on a person: whoever is on the admin list gets Approval Needed in the rail with its count and as the Hosting page's tab, listing every Pending party, hat or no hat, and a Pending party's page opens for them either way. With the hat off that page carries a **Waiting for approval** band with Approve and Hide alone (Hide being how a party is turned down); editing someone else's party, the Admin tab, Hide and Unhide on an open or hidden party, All Parties and a Hidden party's page still wait for the hat. The server keeps enforcing by the admin list regardless.

## Pages

One shell, `web/celebrate/index.html`, routes every page on the client: a left rail (Parties with its three tabs and their counts under it, My Family's Parties with the household under it - Me, then the partner and children, each with how many parties they hold a ticket to - Hosting, Approval Needed for admins, and Host a Party) beside the shared top bar (`docs/toolbar.md`), whose search filters the parties list and carries the words there from any other page.

- **Parties** (`/`, or `/celebrations/{code}` for another year) - the current celebration in a band across the top, then the parties in four tabs: Available and Waitlist (what sells now), All Upcoming (everything still to come, sold out and closed included) and Past Parties, with a category filter. A card is the picture with its audience chip (and a yellow Hosting chip on the viewer's own), the date stamp, the title, subtitle and summary, the household's own tickets on it - everyone in the family and any guests they brought, each behind a ticket (or an hourglass and "waitlist"), on every page the card appears - and a foot with Get Tickets or Join Waitlist and how the room stands: "Tickets available" while there is plenty, then "N tickets left" once a party is getting full - under 40% of its capacity or ten or fewer, whichever comes first, or "N waiting". A family already on a full party - holding a ticket, or waiting - is not asked to join its waitlist: the card's button is Learn More.
- **Party** (`/parties/{id}`, or `/p/{pretty}` when it has a friendly address) - laid out as HCA-Team lays out an event: the wide banner with the date card every app shares floating in its corner (`web/common/datecard.js`; `docs/dev.md`: the day's tile with the sun as it stands, the hours, the place, Add; a party with no date yet says so on a plain stamp) and, opposite, the round tools (the pencil for whoever runs it, and Share - the phone's share sheet, or the link to the clipboard where there is none); the banner itself opens full size on a click; then the chips (a yellow Hosting on the viewer's own, then the audience), the title, the description, and the need-to-know line in the tinted callout - behind a megaphone and "Good to know" unless the host gives it their own emoji and title - beside a rail of cards - the host's invite card first (Who's coming, below); Where, only when there is a street address (signed-in members only), as a map link under the place, and while editing, where the place editor lives (Date & Time too is a row only while editing, or for a party with no date yet); the price and what is left, the hosts by face (a party's minimum to go ahead shows here to its hosts only); the flyer, shown whole and opened full size on a click (hosts upload or replace it right there); and Questions?, a mail to the hosts. Under the words, the ticket band (Get Tickets or Join the Waitlist and what is left; a full party the family already holds a ticket to says "Sold Out - Your Family Has Tickets" and still offers Join the Waitlist, since they may want more; one they wait for says "You're on the Waitlist" over the request itself - who asked, for how many, and their note - with Update Waitlist Request - a host's own family included, since a full party sells nothing more except through Offer on the waitlist; hosts see Add Attendee only once tickets are closed), then My Tickets - the family's own sold tickets, each with Reassign; a waitlist place is not a ticket and sits in the waitlist below instead, marked Your family with Leave waitlist beside it, then Who's Coming as a grid of faces each placed by a line - a student's grade, "Parent to Sam Whitfield (Grade 3)", a staff member's job, "Guest of Jordan Whitfield" - then the waitlist in order. A host's face, and each face on the grid, opens the person's card as HCA-Team opens a volunteer's: the big face, the name with pronouns and what places them, buttons to email, text, call or copy them, the contact rows and the household as chips, and the way through to their Helios Who? page (a guest by name gets the card with just that). Whoever runs the party gets, from an attendee's face, the same card with a second tab, Ticket - who is billed, the price, the note, and a way to reassign it - or, for a waitlist request, how many were asked for and the Offer (where the invoice stands lives in the sheet's INVOICING tab, shown in Admin Tools) - and, above the grid, the attendee contact list: every ticket with the name, a description (a grade, "Parent to Sam Whitfield (Grade 3)", a job, "Guest of Jordan Whitfield"), the person's own address, a student's parents' addresses, who bought the ticket, the status and the note; a click on any column heading copies that column (addresses comma-separated, ready for a mail client), and Copy table takes the whole thing as tab-separated lines for a spreadsheet. Below sits the band of switches with Edit all fields, Add Free Ticket - a host's gift to anyone the party admits, from the directory or by name, minted at $0 so nothing lands on an invoice, with a Guest of picker (any adult in the directory; blank means the host) naming who is bringing them - the ticket sits with that family to pass on, and the note goes to them - and a switch (on by default) to raise the capacity by one so the gift takes no paid place - and, for an admin, Approve / Hide - on a Pending party, Approve and Hide show for an admin with the hat off too, in a band of their own.
- **My Family's Parties** (`/my`) - one section per member of the household with the parties they hold a ticket to or wait for, and one for the guests the family has brought, upcoming first; `/my/{name}` - the address's local part, `/my/ella.whitfield` - is one member's alone. The rail lists them under the heading: My Family (every party anyone in the household is on), Me (the viewer's own page, titled My Parties), then the partner and children by name, each with its count.
- **Hosting** (`/hosting`) - the parties the viewer runs, with counts and money raised; for an admin, also Approval Needed and All Parties.
- **Admin Tools** (`/admin`) - in the admin chrome every app shares: the banner (a preview and Edit Banner), celebrations, categories (renamable, reorderable), settings (the intro under the heading and the note on the ticket form), invoicing (every ticket by purchaser with totals, each openable to mark its invoice sent or paid, and a CSV of the same), and the admin list.

The ticket form asks who to bill (an adult of the household, by face), who is coming (the household by face, with anyone who already holds a ticket or whom the party's audience rules keep out shown greyed and saying why), and a note, and tallies who and how much as it goes; when the room runs out mid-purchase the rest of the family go on the waitlist as one request. Add someone, under the faces, opens a small window for a guest - a full name and, optionally, an email address so the hosts can reach them; a guest with an address the directory does not know is still a guest, billed to the family. Whoever runs the party gets a switch there between Guest and From the directory, a search that names who the party is for ("Search for a student…") and offers only them; those tickets are billed to the person added, or a student's parent.

On phones the rail becomes a drawer behind the hamburger, a slim title strip sits under the shared bar, and a bottom tab bar holds Parties, My Family's Parties and Hosting.

## Editing

As in the other apps: in place, through modals and one-click switches, every change a commit on the store (`docs/storage.md`) - applied to memory at once, written to the sheet and the Change Log behind it on the shared write queue. A change the rules reject is refused before anything is written. Party images are uploaded, found in the image libraries (`internal/imagesearch`), or cropped, like the volunteer portal's.

## Friendly addresses

A party may carry a `Pretty ID`: `fondue` puts it at `celebrate.heliosian.com/p/fondue`, and every link to it and the Share button use that address. Lower-case letters, digits and hyphens, at most 40; one address for one party across every celebration - saving refuses one another party holds. Changing or removing one writes a row to `Redirects`, so the old address keeps working and the browser's address bar is corrected to the live one; the bare `/parties/{id}` always resolves too. Its hosts set it in the editor's Basics tab, an admin too with the hat on.

## Sign-in and install

A link to a party previews in chat apps and social feeds even though the site is behind sign-in: the sign-in page served at a party's address carries the party's Open Graph tags (title; the day and hours, the place in words and the summary as the description; the canonical address), and `/open/share/{id}.png` is a public 1200x630 card drawn on the server by `internal/sharecard`, laid out like HCA-Team's: on the pale wash, the mark and "Helios Celebrate", the title in deep teal with the yellow swoosh, then the day behind a calendar glyph, the hours behind a clock and the place behind a pin; on the right the party's flyer shown whole, or without one its banner scaled to cover the panel. Only an open party previews; a pending or hidden one, or any other page, gets the plain sign-in page and no card. The tags say what a poster on the wall would say - never the street address, never who is coming.

Everything sits behind Google sign-in restricted to the school domain, sharing a session with the other apps on the same tier. It installs to a home screen with the sun-over-the-hills mark under `web/public/celebrate/brand/` (cut from the exports in `~/Dropbox/Kids/Heliosian/images/celebrate/`: the app icon square for the home-screen icons, the bare mark for the favicons, the app switch and the maskables, the vertical lockups for the rail and the login page); there is no splash battery yet.

## Email

The site sends three kinds of mail (`internal/celebrate/mail.go`, over `internal/mail`), each laid out like HCA-Team's: the Helios Celebrate bar, the party's share card as its picture, a heading and a line of explanation, a details box (the party, when, where - the street address too, since the reader is a member - and rows for the occasion), a button to the page and, on any note that says someone is going, Add to Calendar (a Google Calendar link with the party's hours, address and page). Each goes to whoever is billed, with the hosts on Reply-To so a reply reaches a person. A note that says someone is going is followed by a second, short one - **Calendar invite: …** - to the family alone (the same addresses, nobody copied) carrying the party as a calendar invite (`invite.ics`, `METHOD:REQUEST`, from celebrate@reply.heliosian.com as organizer, each recipient an accepted attendee, one `UID` per party and family so a later invite for the same family updates the calendar entry rather than adding a twin), so the hosts copied on the note itself never get an invite - forty tickets would be forty invites on a host's calendar:

- **Your tickets** - after a purchase: who is coming, which tickets sold and which landed on the waitlist, the total ("Free" for a host's gift, with no Billed-to row and no invoicing note), who is billed, the note, and the Ticket Note setting (how invoicing works). The hosts are copied. When someone else took the tickets for the family, they are copied too and the note says who. One ticket for someone other than the purchaser - a child, a guest with an address, a colleague a host added - is that person's note instead: "Sam, you're going!", spoken to them by name, sent to them (a student: to both parents) with the purchaser alongside.
- **You're on the waitlist** - after a waitlist request, new or changed: how many, and that nothing is billed until a host offers places. The hosts are not copied; they get their own note - **Waitlist for …: X wants N tickets** - with the family's note and the party's whole waitlist count, Reply-To set to the family so a host can write straight back, and a button to the party where Offer lives.
- **A place opened up** - when a host moves a name off the waitlist onto a ticket, the hosts copied; the invite follows.

Sending happens off the request and a failure is logged, never shown - the tickets themselves already took. Mail goes out from celebrate@reply.heliosian.com (`CELEBRATE_MAIL_FROM` overrides) through the same Mailgun key as HCA-Team's (`docs/deploy.md`); without it nothing is sent; in sample mode each message is written as an `.html` file under `MAIL_DIR` (default `$TMPDIR/hca-mail`) to open in a browser, its invite as an `-invite.ics` beside it.
