# Helios Loop

Helios Loop is where the community's email groups are made: each is an address at `loop.heliosian.com` whose members follow from rules over the directory, and every message sent to it goes on to those members as they stand when it arrives. It serves at loop.heliosian.com. The sheet is `Groups`, the package is `internal/loop`, and the API is `/api/loop/`.

## Entities

- **Group** — a name, which is the local part of its address and never changes once made; any aliases, other local parts that reach it (`hummingbirds-families` beside `hummingbird-families`), added and removed by a manager at will; a title and a description; whether the title goes in front of every subject (on unless a manager turns it off); who sees it beyond its managers (Visible, below; nobody unless a manager opens it); one or more managers; and its rules. Names and aliases are lowercase letters, digits, dots and hyphens, starting and ending with a letter or digit and never two dots together, unique across every group's name and aliases together, and a handful mail systems reserve (postmaster, abuse, and the like) are refused. A group holds at most ten aliases. Mail to an alias is the group's mail in every way, recorded under the group's name; the group's own address is what the copies name as the list.
- **Rule** — one line of the definition, a filter as Helios Who? has them: a role (Students, Parents, Staff), words to find in a name or address, classrooms, grades, tags and Magic Tags, and Add family, which widens the rule's matches by the parents, children or siblings of the people it matched. Within a rule every choice must hold: Parents and Grade 3 together are the parents of a Grade 3 student, since a parent matches a grade or classroom through their children, as Who?'s filters read them. A rule with no choice at all is refused, so nobody makes an all-community group by accident.
- **Include and exclude** — a group has one or more include rules, whose matches are unioned, and zero or more exclude rules, whose matches are taken away from that. The result is the members' directory addresses; someone whose address is a placeholder nothing can reach is left out.
- **Addition** — someone on the group by hand rather than by rule: an address the directory does not hold (a coach, a league office, a family friend) and the name a manager typed for it, which may be blank. An addition is on the group whatever the rules say, the exclude rules included, until a manager removes it. An address the directory does hold is refused at save with the person's name - those people are on a group by rule - so the two lists stay apart; someone who joins the directory after being added simply becomes a directory member, on the group once. A group may hold two hundred.
- **Excluded** — an address that gets no mail from the group whatever the rules and the additions say, with a note saying why and when it went on: someone who used the Unsubscribe their mail app offers on a message from the group (Mail, below), whose note says so and how; someone on the group who pressed Unsubscribe on its page (Pages, below), whose note says that; or anyone a manager lists by hand with a note of their own. A manager removes an entry in the editor to put the person back, and someone on the group removes their own with Resubscribe on its page, whoever put it there; nothing else does. A group may hold two hundred.
- **Manager** — who may change the group: its rules, its additions, its excluded list, its aliases, its words, its managers, or delete it. Whoever makes a group is its first manager. The app's admins (the `Admins` tab and the platform super admins) see and may change every group. Managers are the app's own notion: the mail goes to exactly the rules' result, and a manager who is not matched by them gets none of it.
- **Suggestion** — a group the viewer could make and has not: a party they host on Helios Celebrate that has not happened yet, or an activity they co-chair on HCA-Team this school year, whose Magic Tag no rule of any group names, whoever wrote the rule. The server works them out for the front page (`Suggested` in `internal/loop/handlers.go`, over the viewer's Magic Tags and every group's rules); each carries the managers the group would start with - the viewer and the party's other hosts or the activity's other co-chairs, those the directory holds - which the Magic Tags carry as their hosts (`Hosts` on `who.List`, filled by `internal/app/lists.go` and never sent to Who?'s client).
- **Archived** — a group one person has put away for themselves: it sits under Archived in their rail and off their front page rather than among their groups, and its Magic Tag stays off their lists in Who? (In Helios Who?, below), though its pages in both apps still open. It is that person's alone - the `Archived` tab holds one row per group per person - and it changes nothing about the group: its address, its rules, its mail and everyone else's view of it are as they were, so anyone who sees a group may archive it (`POST /api/loop/archive`, with `archived` true or false), and the change log is not told.
- **Visible** — who sees a group beyond its managers and the admins, one of three: hidden, the default, so nobody else does; visible to members, so everyone the rules or the additions place on it - whatever the excluded list says - finds it on the front page; or visible to everyone, so anyone signed in to Loop does. Whoever sees a group this way sees its page whole, as a manager does, but changes nothing on it. The one thing a non-manager can do there is on their own behalf: someone the group places on it may take themselves off it and put themselves back, which adds and removes their own entry on the excluded list and nothing else; someone the group does not place on it has no such control.

## Tags are read as their owner

A tag is private to the person who made it and whoever they share it with, and a Magic Tag is a person's own (the party they host, the activity they chair, the band they are room parent for), so a rule naming a tag carries its owner: whoever wrote the rule. The editor offers the editing manager their own tags, the tags shared with them (each named with whose it is), and their Magic Tags. A shared tag in a rule is read as its owner's, and only while the rule's owner is still a manager of it: once the sharing ends, the rule matches nobody through it, and the group's page says the tag is no longer shared. A rule another manager wrote is shown on the group's page and in the editor in words, and can be removed but not changed, since changing it would mean reading their tags. The server enforces the same: a rule read as anyone but the viewer must already be on the group word for word. A Magic Tag never lists its owner (Who? never puts the viewer on their own list), so a host who wants to be on the party's group adds a rule with their own name in it.

## Pages

- **My Groups** (`/`) — the groups the viewer manages, each a card with its title, its address to copy, its description, its member count, and a Visible to members or Visible to everyone chip when it is either; then, under Suggested groups, a card per suggestion (Entities) with the party's or activity's name, a line saying who the group would hold, the managers it would start with and Make this group, which opens the editor filled in (below); then, under Other groups, every group the viewer does not manage but sees (Visible, above), the same cards without the visibility chip. An admin's page is every group. The rail lists the managed groups alone, the ones the viewer has archived under an Archived heading below the rest, folded until the heading is clicked - the choice kept per browser - or while one of them is the page open; on the page they are simply absent. The toolbar's search narrows both sets of cards. New group leads to the editor.
- **A group** (`/groups/{name}`) — a page in tabs under the title, the strip held at the top as the page scrolls and the chosen tab kept in the address's `tab` parameter, so a reload, the editor and the way back from it land on the same tab. Overview: the address to mail or copy, its aliases as a sorted list when it has any, a line saying how subjects go out (with the title in front, or as written), a line saying it is visible to the people on it or to everyone when it is either, a line saying so when the group places the viewer on it but they are on its excluded list, the description, for a manager or an admin a link to its Magic Tag in Helios Who? (below), and the managers. Members, with the count: the members as the rules pick them out now, each with the word that places them and why they are there: every include rule that reached them, said of them ("Tagged in Tech Team", "Student in Hummingbirds"), and whose relative they are when Add family brought them in ("Parent of Mia, student in Hummingbirds"); then the additions, after the directory's people, each "Outside the directory" and "Added by hand", with no photo and no link into Who?, having no page there. Rules: the rules read out in words. Excluded, with the count: each entry with its note and the day it went on. History, for a manager or an admin: every message that went out to the group, newest first, with its sender, its subject, when it came and how many copies went, asked of the server (`GET /api/loop/messages`) when the tab first opens and read from memory as the server records it, so a message shows the moment it goes out and its trouble the moment Mailgun reports it; a message some of whose copies bounced, were put off or were marked as spam carries a chip counting each, which opens to the addresses with the provider's words. The editor's preview says the same for the draft as the Members tab. Edit, for a manager or an admin, opens the editor on the same page. Beside it, for anyone the group places on it, Unsubscribe - or Resubscribe when their address is on the excluded list - does the one thing and reloads the page; whoever sees the page but is not on the group has neither. Archive - or Unarchive once it is - is there for anyone who sees the page (Archived, above), and an archived group's Overview says so.
- **The editor** (`/new`, or a group's page with `?edit=1`) — the same tabs as the page, Rules ahead of Members since the rules are what the editor is for, every field kept in the one draft whichever tab shows. Opened from a suggestion (`/new?from=<Magic Tag key>`) it starts filled in: the party's or activity's name as the title, the address suggested from it, the suggestion's managers, and one include rule, anyone tagged in that Magic Tag plus their parents - no role picked, so a student on the list brings their parents along and everyone else on it is on the group as themselves - so the members preview shows who the group would reach before anything is saved; every field is the viewer's to change, and nothing exists until Make the group. Overview: the title, the address (typed once, suggested from the title, fixed after), Also answers as - the aliases, each a row with Remove, and a box and Add under them, refused at save when another group already has the name or alias - the description, the subject switch (Put the title in front of every subject, on by default, with a line saying what that means for this title), Who sees the group (Hidden, Visible to members or Visible to everyone, hidden by default, with a line saying who sees the group and what they can do on it), the managers picked from the directory, and Delete group. Rules: the include and exclude rules each a row of controls (role chips, a search box, Classroom, Grade, Tags and Add family dropdowns), and Outside the directory - the additions, each a row with Remove, and a name box, an address box and Add under them. Excluded: each entry a row with its note and Remove, and an address box, a note box and Add under them. History, on an existing group, as on the page. Members: a preview of the members the draft picks out, rules, additions and the excluded list alike, asked of the server as any of them changes so the client never reimplements matching. On an existing group the preview also says what saving does: who joins and who leaves, by name, above the list, with a Joins chip on each newcomer in it and each leaver greyed at its end, or that the members stay as they are. Every member list, the preview's included, runs its full length and the page scrolls; Save and Cancel sit in a bar stuck to the foot of the window while the editor is open, so they are in reach on every tab, and on an existing group the bar counts who joins and who leaves, so a rule changed on Rules shows its effect without a trip to Members. Saving a new group makes its address live at once, since the address is the sheet's row; deleting one, after a word of confirmation, means mail to the address bounces.
- **Admin Tools** (`/admin`) — the Admins list, and Appearance for the platform's super admins, in the chrome every app shares.

## In Helios Who?

Every group a person manages is a Magic Tag of theirs in Who? (`docs/who/directory.md`, Lists): its members as the rules pick them out now, less the person themselves as every Magic Tag leaves the viewer off, with the same Profiles, Email List and Map views and the CSV as any list, and the list's page linking back to the group here; a group the person has archived (Archived, above) keeps its list, off Who?'s rail and Tags filter for them but open from the group's page here. Its additions are the list's guests, as a party's ticket holders the directory has no record for are a party list's: a GUEST card after the directory's people, a row in the Email List and the CSV with the address, and a small page of their own saying which group they are on, and never in the combined results of the Tags filter, on Invites, on the Map or in a saved tag. Every directory name on a group's page here links to that person's page in Who?. A group's rule can never name a group, so the Magic Tags flow one way, from the groups into the directory.

## Mail

Mail to a group's address is received by Mailgun and forwarded by the server to every member, one copy each, with nobody's address on it but the group's and the sender's: a member never learns who else is on the group, or what other groups exist, from the mail. Anyone may post; there is no moderation. What arrives is kept whole (`data.md`, Mail) before anything goes out.

**The copy each member gets** is the message as sent, its body and attachments untouched, its `Message-ID`, `In-Reply-To` and `References` kept so every member's copy is the same message and replies thread, its `To` and `Cc` as the sender wrote them, and a few headers rewritten. `From` becomes the sender's name "via" the group's title at the group's address - `Alice Smith via Soccer Team Families <soccer-team@loop.heliosian.com>` - since the sender's own domain will not vouch for a message it did not send (DMARC), and Mailgun signs only for `loop.heliosian.com`; `Reply-To` is the sender's own address (or the Reply-To they set), so a plain reply goes back to them and reply-all reaches them and the group, and `X-Original-From` carries the From as it was. The subject gets `[Title] ` in front, once, kept in front of a `Fwd:` and behind a `Re:`, so a reply to a prefixed subject is `Re: [Title] …` and never `[Title] Re: [Title] …`; a manager who turns the switch off gets subjects as written. Every copy carries `List-Id`, `List-Post`, `Precedence: list`, a Loop marker, and its recipient's own `List-Unsubscribe` in both forms, a mail address and a one-click link, with `List-Unsubscribe-Post` beside it.

**Unsubscribe** is that header: a mail app that offers an Unsubscribe button posts to the link or writes to the address, and a person who follows the link by hand lands on a small page naming the group and their address with one button. Whichever way, they go on the group's excluded list (Entities) with a note saying which way, and get nothing more from it; the page says so, and says a manager is the way back. The link and the address's subject both carry a token signed with the group and the address, so neither can be made up or bent to another person. Whether a mail app shows the button is the app's own call: Gmail and Fastmail both withhold it from senders they have no history with, headers or no headers, and Loop adds no footer to make up for that.

**What is not forwarded**: a message already sent through Loop (the marker, so two groups that hold each other's address cannot ring), auto-submitted mail such as vacation replies, and a mail system's own notices. Each is kept and recorded as dropped, with why.

**Delivery trouble**: Mailgun tells the server when a copy bounces for good, is put off for now, or is complained of, and each is recorded against the group, the address and the message's `Message-ID` (`data.md`) and shown under that message in the group's History, so a manager sees an address that has gone dead.

## Sample data

`sampledata/groups/` holds three groups: one over a tag with Add family, with a coach and a league office added by hand from outside the directory, one with an include and an exclude rule over grades, visible to everyone, and one managed by someone other than the sample parent, who sees it only because the sample Config sheet makes them a super admin; one sent message on the soccer team, and one bounce of it, on the league office, so History and its chip have something to show. In sample mode nothing receives mail: the routes say they are not set up, and a forward, were one to run, would land as `.eml` files beside the other sample mail.

## Brand

The app wears Heliosian's marks as stand-ins under `web/public/loop/brand/` and as its mark in the app switch (`web/public/common/brand/apps/loop.png`), and Who?'s outline as the mark beside its Magic Tags in Who?'s sidebar (`loop-outline.png` there), until it has art of its own.
