# Helios Loop

Helios Loop is where the community's email groups are made: each is an address at `loop.heliosian.com` whose members follow from rules over the directory, and every message sent to it goes on to those members as they stand when it arrives. It serves at loop.heliosian.com. The sheet is `Groups`, the package is `internal/loop`, and the API is `/api/loop/`.

## Entities

- **Group** — a name, which is the local part of its address and never changes once made; any aliases, other local parts that reach it (`hummingbirds-families` beside `hummingbird-families`), added and removed by a manager at will; a title and a description; whether the title goes in front of every subject (on unless a manager turns it off); who sees it beyond its managers (Visible, below; nobody unless a manager opens it); who can post to it and who can reply to what it sends (Posting and Replying, below; anyone unless a manager narrows them); one or more managers; and its rules. Names and aliases are lowercase letters, digits, dots and hyphens, starting and ending with a letter or digit and never two dots together, unique across every group's name and aliases together, and a handful mail systems reserve (postmaster, abuse, and the like) are refused. A group holds at most ten aliases. Mail to an alias is the group's mail in every way, recorded under the group's name; the group's own address is what the copies name as the list.
- **Rule** — one line of the definition, a filter as Helios Who? has them: a role (Students, Parents, Staff), words to find in a name or address, classrooms, grades, tags and Magic Tags, and Add family, which widens the rule's matches by the parents, children or siblings of the people it matched. Within a rule every choice must hold, read as Who?'s tag page reads the same choices: the words, classrooms, grades and tags pick people out, Add family brings in their relatives, and the role then keeps only people of that kind - so Parents tagged in Carpool plus their parents is the parents of the tagged children as well as the tagged parents, never the children (the editor reads it out as "Anyone tagged in Carpool, plus their parents, keeping parents only"). Parents and Grade 3 together are the parents of a Grade 3 student, since a parent matches a grade or classroom through their children, as Who?'s filters read them. A rule with no choice at all is refused, so nobody makes an all-community group by accident.
- **Include and exclude** — a group has one or more include rules, whose matches are unioned, and zero or more exclude rules, whose matches are taken away from that. The result is the members' directory addresses; someone whose address is a placeholder nothing can reach is left out.
- **Addition** — someone on the group by hand rather than by rule: an address the directory does not hold (a coach, a league office, a family friend) and the name a manager typed for it, which may be blank. An addition is on the group whatever the rules say, the exclude rules included, until a manager removes it. An address the directory does hold is refused at save with the person's name - those people are on a group by rule - so the two lists stay apart; someone who joins the directory after being added simply becomes a directory member, on the group once. A group may hold two hundred.
- **Excluded** — an address that gets no mail from the group whatever the rules and the additions say, with a note saying why and when it went on: someone who used the Unsubscribe their mail app offers on a message from the group (Mail, below), whose note says so and how; someone on the group who pressed Unsubscribe on its page (Pages, below), whose note says that; or anyone a manager lists by hand with a note of their own. A manager removes an entry in the editor to put the person back, and someone on the group removes their own with Resubscribe on its page, whoever put it there; nothing else does. A group may hold two hundred.
- **Manager** — who may change the group: its rules, its additions, its excluded list, its aliases, its words, its managers, or delete it. Whoever makes a group is its first manager. The app's admins (the `Admins` tab and the platform super admins) see and may change every group. Managers are the app's own notion: the mail goes to exactly the rules' result, and a manager who is not matched by them gets none of it.
- **Suggestion** — a group the viewer could make and has not: a tag of their own in Who? that holds someone and that no group's rule names (a rule naming a tag by its owner's address, another person's tag of the same name is no reason to leave theirs out); a party they host on Helios Celebrate that has not happened yet, or an activity they co-chair on HCA-Team this school year, whose Magic Tag no rule of any group names, whoever wrote the rule. The server works them out for the front page (`SuggestedTags` and `Suggested` in `internal/loop/handlers.go`, over the viewer's tags, their Magic Tags and every group's rules), the tags first by name; each carries the managers the group would start with - the viewer alone for a tag, and for the others the viewer and the party's other hosts or the activity's other co-chairs, those the directory holds - which the Magic Tags carry as their hosts (`Hosts` on `who.List`, filled by `internal/app/lists.go` and never sent to Who?'s client).
- **Archived** — a group one person has put away for themselves: it sits under Archived in their rail and off their front page rather than among their groups, and its Magic Tag stays off their lists in Who? (In Helios Who?, below), though its pages in both apps still open. It is that person's alone - the `Archived` tab holds one row per group per person - and it changes nothing about the group: its address, its rules, its mail and everyone else's view of it are as they were, so anyone who sees a group may archive it (`POST /api/loop/archive`, with `archived` true or false), and the change log is not told.
- **Visible** — who sees a group beyond its managers and the admins, one of three: hidden, the default, so nobody else does; visible to members, so everyone the rules or the additions place on it - whatever the excluded list says - finds it on the front page; or visible to everyone, so anyone signed in to Loop does. Whoever sees a group this way sees its members and its description, not the rules that choose them (A rule discloses the tags it names, below), and changes nothing on it. The one thing a non-manager can do there is on their own behalf: someone the group places on it may take themselves off it and put themselves back, which adds and removes their own entry on the excluded list and nothing else; someone the group does not place on it has no such control.
- **Posting** — whose new messages to the group go out, one of three: everyone, the default, so mail from any address does; members, so mail from the group's managers and from everyone the rules or the additions place on it - whatever the excluded list says - does; or managers, so the managers' alone does. The sender is the address in the message's `From`, read through the directory's aliases once the mail has proved it (Mail, below); anyone else's mail is dropped and they are told so. The app's admins post as anyone else does.
- **Replying** — whose replies to the group's own mail go out, the same three choices, everyone by default, read the same way. A reply is a message whose `In-Reply-To` or `References` names the `Message-ID` of a message this group sent; a reply to a reply that went out counts too, so a whole thread does. A reply is judged by Replying alone and anything else by Posting alone, so a group only its managers post to can still take its members' replies, and a group anyone may write to can keep its threads to its members. A message ID is on every copy the group sent, so anyone who holds one can reply as far as Replying lets them.

## A rule discloses the tags it names

A tag is private to the person who made it and whoever they let manage it, and a Magic Tag is a person's own (the party they host, the activity they chair, the band they are room parent for). A rule naming one discloses it: whoever sees the thing the rule is on sees who the tag holds, so naming a tag in a rule is the tag's owner's call, or a manager's, and nobody else's. The rule names the tag absolutely - the owner's address and the name - and carries no owner of its own; the same holds wherever the filter is read (`internal/filter`): Loop's groups, Heliosian's audiences (`docs/home/data.md`, Rules) and When's guest lists (`docs/calendar/calendar.md`, Add Group).

- **Writing one.** The editor offers the manager their own tags, the tags shared with them (each named with whose it is), and their Magic Tags, and the server refuses a rule naming anything else (`filter.Writable`) - with one message whether the tag is someone else's or does not exist, so trying names learns nothing. A rule already on the group word for word passes whoever saves, so a colleague's rule survives an edit to the rest; the editor reads such a rule out with a note and lets it be removed but not opened, since any change would be a new rule of the viewer's naming that tag. Removing a rule is always allowed.
- **Reading one.** A tag reads while one of the group's managers owns it or is among its managers, and a Magic Tag while one of them has it (`Editors` on `filter.List`, the group's managers): a rule nobody who edits the group could write today matches nobody, and the editor says the tag is no longer shared. So when a tag's owner stops sharing it, every group it was named in through that manager goes quiet unless another manager of the group may still read it; the owner's remedies beyond that are a word to the group's managers, or renaming or deleting the tag, which every rule then reads as no longer a tag. An admin saving a rule on a group they do not manage is held to the same line: a tag of their own that none of the group's managers can read is refused.
- **Seeing one.** The rules and why each member is on go to the group's managers and the admins alone; whoever else sees the group sees its members and its description, and nothing of how they were chosen.

A party's or an event's Magic Tag lists its hosts or co-chairs along with everyone else, its owner among them, so a group made of it reaches whoever is running the thing; a group's Magic Tag lists the group's members, which its manager is only by rule.

## Pages

- **My Groups** (`/`) — the groups the viewer manages, each a card with its title, its address to copy, its description, its member count, and a Visible to members or Visible to everyone chip when it is either; then, under Suggested groups, a card per suggestion (Entities) wearing the mark of the app it comes from, with the tag's, party's or activity's name, a line saying who the group would hold, the managers it would start with and Make this group, which opens the editor filled in (below); then, under Other groups, every group the viewer does not manage but sees (Visible, above), the same cards without the visibility chip. An admin's page is every group. The rail lists the managed groups alone, the ones the viewer has archived under an Archived heading below the rest, folded until the heading is clicked - the choice kept per browser - or while one of them is the page open; on the page they are simply absent. The toolbar's search narrows both sets of cards. New group opens the editor's window over the page.
- **A group** (`/groups/{name}`) — above the tabs, what the group is: the address to mail or copy, with a quiet count of its aliases at the band's right that shows them on hover or focus, a line saying how subjects go out (with the group name in front, or as written), a line saying it is visible to the people on it or to everyone when it is either, a line saying only its managers and the people on it, or only its managers, can post new messages when it is either, a line saying who can reply to its messages when that is not who can post, a line saying so when the viewer has archived it, a line saying so when the group places the viewer on it but they are on its excluded list, and the description. Then tabs, the strip held at the top as the page scrolls and the chosen tab kept in the address's `tab` parameter, so a reload lands on the same tab. Members, first and open by default, with the count: the members as the rules pick them out now, drawn as Celebrate's Who's Coming grid draws them - a tile each with the square photo or the name's first letter, a student's grade on its corner in Who?'s colour for the grade as HCA-Team badges it, the name and a line placing them (a student's grade, a staff member's job, a parent's children with their grades) - each opening the person's page in Who?, its tooltip saying why they are there: every include rule that reached them ("Tagged in Tech Team", "Student in Hummingbirds"), and whose relative they are when Add family brought them in ("Parent of Mia, student in Hummingbirds"); the additions after the directory's people, each a Guest tile with no page in Who? to open; a box above narrows the grid by name, address or that line; under it, for a manager or an admin, the rules read out in words - a green plus before each include rule, a red minus before each exclude, a count of the excluded addresses after, and a pencil that opens the editor (below) on its Members tab - and for anyone else nothing, the tooltips saying nothing either (A rule discloses the tags it names). Managers, with the count: the managers, each a card across with the face, the name and Subscribed or Not subscribed - whether the rules place them on the group, since a manager gets its mail only then - with a pencil for a manager or an admin that opens the card for changes (a check to close it), a remove on every card but the last and a picker to add one, each change saved at once. History, for a manager or an admin: every message that went out to the group, newest first, with its sender, its subject and when it came, asked of the server (`GET /api/loop/messages`) when the tab first opens and read from memory as the server records it, a few seconds behind Mailgun. At each message's right, a chip counts its copies as they stand: delivered, failed and pending. A copy Mailgun has put off is still pending, not failed, and one delivered after a delay is simply delivered, so the chip says nothing about a delay. The chip opens to every copy, failed first, then pending, then delivered, each a row with the person, Delivered or Failed and when, or Pending. Under a copy that met anything but a plain delivery - a delay, a bounce, a spam complaint - is its list of attempts, each with when and the provider's words, so a pending copy's delay shows as a note under it. Edit, beside the title for a manager or an admin, opens the editor in a window over the page - a box in the middle of the screen with the title as its heading, typed in place, and a close, the editor inside with its Save bar stuck to the box's foot, its tabs kept to itself rather than the address - so the page stays where it is; Save, Delete, Cancel, the close and Escape shut it, a click outside does not, and the page reloads after a save, moving to the front page after a delete. Beside the title, for anyone the group places on it, Unsubscribe - or Resubscribe when their address is on the excluded list - does the one thing and reloads the page; whoever sees the page but is not on the group has neither. Archive - or Unarchive once it is - is there for anyone who sees the page (Archived, above).

## In Helios Who?

Every group a person manages is a Magic Tag of theirs in Who? (`docs/who/directory.md`, Lists): its members as the rules pick them out now, less the person themselves as every Magic Tag leaves the viewer off, with the same Profiles, Email List and Map views and the CSV as any list, and the list's page linking back to the group here; a group the person has archived (Archived, above) keeps its list, off Who?'s rail and Tags filter for them but open at its address. Its additions are the list's guests, as a party's ticket holders the directory has no record for are a party list's: a GUEST card after the directory's people, a row in the Email List and the CSV with the address, and a small page of their own saying which group they are on, and never in the combined results of the Tags filter, on Invites, on the Map or in a saved tag. Every directory name on a group's page here links to that person's page in Who?. A group's rule can never name a group, so the Magic Tags flow one way, from the groups into the directory.

## Mail

Mail to a group's address is received by Mailgun and forwarded by the server to every member, one copy each, with nobody's address on it but the group's and the sender's: a member never learns who else is on the group, or what other groups exist, from the mail. Who may post is the group's Posting, and who may reply its Replying (Entities); there is no moderation. What arrives is kept whole (`data.md`, Mail) before anything goes out.

**The copy each member gets** is the message as sent, its body and attachments untouched, its `Message-ID`, `In-Reply-To` and `References` kept so every member's copy is the same message and replies thread, its `To` and `Cc` as the sender wrote them, and a few headers rewritten. `From` becomes the sender's name "via" the group's title at the group's address - `Alice Smith via Soccer Team Families <soccer-team@loop.heliosian.com>` - since the sender's own domain will not vouch for a message it did not send (DMARC), and Mailgun signs only for `loop.heliosian.com`; `Reply-To` is the sender's own address (or the Reply-To they set), so a plain reply goes back to them and reply-all reaches them and the group, and `X-Original-From` carries the From as it was. The subject gets `[Title] ` in front, once, kept in front of a `Fwd:` and behind a `Re:`, so a reply to a prefixed subject is `Re: [Title] …` and never `[Title] Re: [Title] …`; a manager who turns the switch off gets subjects as written. Every copy carries `List-Id`, `List-Post`, `Precedence: list`, a Loop marker, and its recipient's own `List-Unsubscribe` in both forms, a mail address and a one-click link, with `List-Unsubscribe-Post` beside it.

**Unsubscribe** is that header: a mail app that offers an Unsubscribe button posts to the link or writes to the address, and a person who follows the link by hand lands on a small page naming the group and their address with one button. Whichever way, they go on the group's excluded list (Entities) with a note saying which way, and get nothing more from it; the page says so, and says a manager is the way back. The link and the address's subject both carry a token signed with the group and the address, so neither can be made up or bent to another person. Whether a mail app shows the button is the app's own call: Gmail and Fastmail both withhold it from senders they have no history with, headers or no headers, and Loop adds no footer to make up for that.

**Authentication**: every post has to prove its `From`. Mailgun puts an `Authentication-Results` header on top of what it receives, with its DKIM, SPF and DMARC verdicts, and a post goes on only when DMARC passed for the `From`'s domain, or DKIM or SPF passed for a domain aligned with it - the same domain, or one inside the other. Only the topmost such header counts, and only when Mailgun wrote it (`mxa.mailgun.org` and its kin); one further down is the sender's own and is ignored. A post relayed through a Google Group arrives re-signed as the group's own domain, which no longer proves its `From`, so when Mailgun's header also says `arc=pass` - the ARC chain of forwarders' seals is intact - the newest ARC set is read instead: if `google.com` sealed it, its `ARC-Authentication-Results`, Google's verdicts on the post as it first arrived, are held to the same test. No other forwarder's seal is trusted.

**What is not forwarded**: a message already sent through Loop (the marker, so two groups that hold each other's address cannot ring), auto-submitted mail such as vacation replies, a mail system's own notices, mail that fails authentication, and mail from a sender the group's Posting leaves out, or for a reply its Replying. Each is recorded as dropped, with why. The last gets a notice back at its `From`, from `HCA-Team <team@loop.heliosian.com>` - the `team` group, so a reply to it reaches people - threaded under the post, saying it was not sent and who can post, or reply; it carries `Auto-Submitted: auto-replied` and the Loop marker so nothing answers it in turn. The rest get nothing: a post that fails authentication has no sender to trust with an answer.

**Delivery**: each copy is recorded as it goes out, and Mailgun tells the server when a copy is delivered, bounces for good, is put off for now, or is complained of. Each is recorded against the group, the address and the message's `Message-ID` (`data.md`) and shown under that message in the group's History, so a manager sees who has the message, who is still waiting on it and whose address has gone dead.

## Sample data

`sampledata/groups/` holds three groups: one over a tag with Add family, with a coach and a league office added by hand from outside the directory, one with an include and an exclude rule over grades, visible to everyone and open to its managers' posts alone and its members' replies, and one managed by someone other than the sample parent, who sees it only because the sample Config sheet makes them a super admin; one sent message on the soccer team, with its five copies delivered, delivered after a delay, still pending after a delay, and bounced on the league office, so History and its chip have something to show. In sample mode nothing receives mail: the routes say they are not set up, and a forward, were one to run, would land as `.eml` files beside the other sample mail.
