# Data model

The tabs, columns, and validation rules are in `internal/loop`; this file carries only what reading that code cannot tell you.

The groups live in one Google Sheet, `Groups`, in the community shared drive, reached through drive membership like the other sheets. `GROUPS_SHEET` names it, and `cmd/createtabs` reads that variable to lay it out from an empty spreadsheet titled `Groups`, or to add the tabs and columns an older one lacks.

## Tabs

- `Groups` — Name, Title, Description, Created By, Created, Subject Prefix, Visible. One row per group. Name is the address's local part and the key of every other tab. Subject Prefix is `off` for a group whose subjects go out as written; blank, or anything else, means the title goes in front. Visible is `everyone` for a group everyone in Loop sees, `members` for one the people on it see, and `hidden` or blank for one its managers and the admins alone see (`loop.md`, Visible); any other value refuses the load.
- `Managers` — Group, Email. One row per manager per group; every group needs at least one.
- `Rules` — Group, Kind, Roles, Search, Classrooms, Grades, Tags, Family, Owner. One row per rule, in the order the editor shows them. Kind is `include` or `exclude`. Roles, Classrooms, Grades, Tags and Family are comma-separated lists; Tags holds tag names, Magic Tag keys (`party:<id>`, `activity:<id>`, `room:<band>`) and shared tags (`shared:<owner's address>:<name>`, one the owner has let the rule's Owner manage) alike, read as Owner's. A tag with a comma in its name cannot be used in a rule. Every group needs at least one include rule, and every rule at least one of the facets.
- `Additions` — Group, Email, Name. One row per person added by hand from outside the directory (`loop.md`, Addition). Email is lowercased and must be an address; Name may be blank, the address standing in for it. A group holds at most two hundred, once each by address. The load never asks the directory about them, so an address that has since joined the directory is not a refused load; the app refuses one at save alone.
- `Excluded` — Group, Email, Note, Timestamp. One row per address kept off a group (`loop.md`, Excluded): the address, lowercased; a note saying why, which an unsubscribe fills in itself (`Unsubscribed by one-click`, `by the page`, `by mail from <address>`, `by the group's page in Loop`) and a manager types by hand, up to a hundred and twenty characters; and when it went on. A group holds at most two hundred, once each by address. The evaluator leaves them off whatever the rules and additions say; a manager's Remove in the editor deletes the row, as does the person's own Resubscribe on the group's page (`POST /api/loop/subscription`, which adds the row with `subscribed` false and deletes it with `subscribed` true, for someone the rules or additions place on a group they can see).
- `Aliases` — Group, Alias. One row per other local part a group answers as (`loop.md`, Group), each held to the same form as a name and unique across every group's name and aliases: a load with an alias that is another group's name or alias, or a group named for another's alias, refuses. At most ten per group.
- `Messages` — ID, Group, Received, From, Subject, State, Recipients, Object, Detail, Source, Message ID. One row per message per group it was addressed to, keyed by ID (the key of Mailgun's stored copy) and Group: when it came, who from, the subject, where it stands (Mail, below), how many copies went out, the archive object holding it whole, what went wrong or why it was dropped, the address the stored copy is fetched from, and the message's own `Message-ID` without its angle brackets, filled when it is archived. Written by the server alone; read back to resume what a restart cut short and, for the rows `sent`, as the group's History (`loop.md`, Pages). The archive it indexes is the record.
- `Deliveries` — Timestamp, Group, Email, Event, Message, Detail. One row per copy Mailgun could not deliver as sent: which group's, to whom, what happened (`bounced`, `delivery_delayed`, `complained`), the copy's Message-ID, and Mailgun's words. Appended by the server, never edited. History shows each under the `Messages` row whose Message ID is the same, angle brackets aside; the copies Loop sends keep the sender's `Message-ID`, which is what makes the two meet. A row with no such message - one from a message that carried no `Message-ID`, or one sent before the column - is shown nowhere.
- `Admins` — Email. Who sees and may change every group, beyond the platform super admins (`docs/config.md`).
- `Archived` — Group, Email. One row per person per group they have archived for themselves (`loop.md`, Archived): the address, lowercased. Archive on the group's page adds the row and Unarchive deletes it (`POST /api/loop/archive`); a row naming a group the sheet does not have refuses the load, like every other tab's.
- `Change Log` — Timestamp, Actor, Action, Group, Detail. Appended on every save, delete, unsubscribe and resubscribe; never read back.

**A load either succeeds whole or refuses**, the stance every app takes: a group with no manager or no include rule, a rule, addition, exclusion or alias naming a group the sheet does not have, an unknown kind, role, relation or visibility, a name or alias that cannot be an address, or an alias two groups would share refuses the load, so a hand edit that breaks a rule surfaces as a refused start or a failed refresh rather than a group quietly matching nobody. The app checks the same rules before it writes, so nothing it saves can refuse the next load. `Messages` and `Deliveries` are records, not rules: a row of either naming a group since deleted is kept and ignored.

**Saving rewrites a group's rows whole.** A save upserts the group's `Groups` row and replaces its `Managers`, `Rules`, `Additions`, `Excluded` and `Aliases` rows, so the sheet's rows for a group are always exactly what the editor last held. A delete removes all six and the group's `Archived` rows; its messages and deliveries stay.

## The membership

A group's members are never stored. They are computed from the directory model, the owner's tags (`Cache.Tags`, or `who.TagsOf` over the tab for a tool), the tags shared with them (`Cache.SharedTags`, or `who.SharedTagsOf`) and their Magic Tags (`app.SmartLists` plus the directory's room-parent lists) whenever anything asks: the group's page, the editor's preview, every forward and `cmd/loadcheck` all call the one evaluator in `internal/loop/eval.go`, a port of Who?'s client-side filter with the same reading of a parent's grade and classroom through their children. The rules' result is then unioned with the group's additions, each on once by address whatever the exclude rules say, with `Added` as its reason, and the excluded addresses taken away last. The tests pin it to the sample community.

The group's Magic Tag in Who? (`GroupLists` in `internal/app/lists.go`) sorts the same members by whether the directory holds them: the directory's people are the list's People, and the rest are its Guests under the name the manager typed, keyed `<group name>:<address>` - the one `who.Guest` shape a party's ticket holders outside the directory use, keyed by the ticket there.

## Mail

**Why Mailgun.** Loop needs a sender that takes a message as bytes and passes it on as they are: `To` and `Cc` as the sender wrote them, so reply-all reaches the group, and the sender's `Message-ID`, so every member's copy is one message and replies thread. Resend, which every other app sends through, rebuilds every message on both its API and its SMTP relay - `To` becomes the envelope recipient, `Cc` is dropped, `Message-ID` and `Date` are replaced, and a `List-Unsubscribe` with two forms is corrupted - and has no setting for it. Mailgun's `messages.mime` endpoint delivered a probe byte for byte, adding only its own `Return-Path`, a `Sender`, two `X-Mailgun` headers and a DKIM signature that covers the list headers. Amazon SES, which Resend runs on, keeps `To` and `Cc` on a raw send but documents replacing `Message-ID`.

**Receiving.** `loop.heliosian.com` has to be a domain in Mailgun, verified for sending, with its MX records pointing at Mailgun, so that every message to any address under it lands there, and needs one route matching every recipient under the domain that stores the message, `store(notify="https://loop.heliosian.com/hooks/mail")` - `docs/deploy.md` says how to read what the account holds. On a message, Mailgun keeps the message for three days and calls the route with the recipient it matched, the sender, the From, the subject, and the `message-url` the stored copy is fetched from, signed with the HTTP webhook signing key (`MAILGUN_WEBHOOK_KEY`; `docs/deploy.md`) - the HMAC-SHA256 of timestamp and token, which `mail.VerifyMailgun` checks, allowing a quarter of an hour of clock drift. The call comes as JSON or as a form and the handler reads either. A recipient that is a group's name or alias in the sheet becomes a job under the group's name, one job however many of its addresses the message named; `unsubscribe@loop.heliosian.com` is the mail form of unsubscribing (below); anything else under the domain is logged and ignored. The handler answers 200 for what it took and 406 for a signature that does not check, which Mailgun does not retry; any other answer it retries for eight hours.

**The pipeline.** For each group a message was sent to, the handler records a `Messages` row in state `received`, the `message-url` in Source, and answers at once, so Mailgun never waits on the work; a route fires once per matched recipient and a retried call is the same notification again, and the row, with an in-memory record of what this process has handled, is what makes a second delivery do nothing. One worker (`mailer` in `internal/loop/mail.go`) then takes the messages in turn: fetches the stored message from its Source, raw (`Accept: message/rfc2822`, the `body-mime` field, `mail.Mailgun.Stored`); drops it if it is one Loop already sent, auto-submitted or a mail system's notice (state `dropped`, with why); writes it whole to the mail bucket as `loop/<group>/<time>-<id>.eml` (state `stored`, the object and the message's `Message-ID` named in the row); rewrites the headers (`loop.md`, Mail; `rewrite.go`); works out the members as they stand; and posts Mailgun one copy per member to `messages.mime`, the message as bytes with that one member as the envelope recipient. The row ends `sent` with the count of copies that went, and any that failed named in Detail, or `failed` when none did or an earlier step failed, with the step and the error. A restart re-queues every row still `received` or `stored`, fetching from Source again, so a message the instance died on goes out on the next start as long as Mailgun still holds it; a `failed` row is a person's to look at, and a message can be resent by hand from Mailgun's logs or by posting again.

**Delivery trouble.** The domain's Mailgun webhooks for permanent failure, temporary failure and complaint have to point at `POST /hooks/events` (public, the same signature, the signature object inside the JSON; `docs/deploy.md`, again, for reading where they point). Each names the copy's From - the group's address, which says which group - and its recipient, and is appended to `Deliveries` as `bounced`, `delivery_delayed` or `complained`; an event for a From that is not under Loop's domain is not Loop's and is ignored.

**The archive.** Every message forwarded or dropped is in `gs://heliosian-mail` (`docs/deploy.md`), whole, as received, under the group and the time; nothing here serves it yet. `Messages` is its index. Mailgun's own three-day copy is the working store, not the record.

**Unsubscribe.** Every copy's `List-Unsubscribe` names both forms: `mailto:unsubscribe@loop.heliosian.com?subject=<token>` and `https://loop.heliosian.com/open/unsubscribe/<token>`, the token the group name and the recipient's address signed with the session key (`internal/loop/unsubscribe.go`), with `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` beside them. `GET` on the link is the page with the button; `POST`, from the button or from a mail app's one-click, adds the `Excluded` row with a note saying which way and applies it in memory at once, and answers an empty 200 to a one-click and the page to the button. Mail to the address comes through the same route as a post, and its subject, less any `Re:`, is the token; the sender is recorded but not checked, since the token reaches only the one person's copy. All of it is public (`internal/auth`); the signature is the whole of the check.

**Every other app** sends through Mailgun too, by its JSON endpoint (`mail.Mailgun.Send`), and the calendar's replies come in through a Mailgun route at `reply.heliosian.com` the same way Loop's posts do; Resend is gone (`docs/deploy.md`).

`cmd/loadcheck` prints every group with its member count, its unsubscribed count, its subject switch and its visibility.
